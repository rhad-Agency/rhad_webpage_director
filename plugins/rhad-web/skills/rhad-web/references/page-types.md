# Page types and blueprints

Ask which page type before planning. Each blueprint is a starting order, not a template to fill: every section needs a job, real content and its own composition. Cut sections that have none.

| Type | Job | Typical visitor | Primary action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Service page** | Explain one service well enough to enquire | Searched or referred, comparing agencies | Enquire or arrange a call |
| **Campaign or ad landing page** | Convert one audience on one offer | Arrived from an ad or message, one decision | The single form or WhatsApp |
| **Product page** (RHAD45) | Show a product working and the price | SME owner with a recurring problem | Arrange a review |
| **Grant or programme page** (EDGE) | Explain eligibility and process conditionally | SME owner checking whether they qualify | Check eligibility, enquire |
| **Homepage** | Say what RHAD is and route people | Mixed intent | Start a conversation |
| **Microsite or event page** | One time-boxed story | Invited or following a campaign | RSVP or enquire |
| **Pricing or enquiry page** | Remove doubt and capture the lead | Ready to act | Submit the form |

## Service page blueprint

1. **Hero**: the situation named plainly, who it is for, the primary action, one signature visual. No second headline.
2. **The problem in the customer's words**: two or three concrete situations (a missed WhatsApp enquiry, a campaign nobody reports on). Specific beats broad.
3. **What RHAD does about it**: the service as a definition list or slip. What is included, what is not, who does what.
4. **How it works**: a real ordered process with an owner per step and a time frame only if confirmed.
5. **Proof**: real client work or quotes with permission, labelled. If there is none, say less. Do not pad.
6. **Price or scope signal**: what drives cost, a starting point only if confirmed. "Scope and cost are confirmed before you proceed."
7. **FAQ**: the real objections, three to six, answers that stand alone.
8. **Conversion**: the form or WhatsApp, what happens next, response window only if true.
9. **Footer**: parent link, privacy link that exists, credits.

## Campaign landing page blueprint

One audience, one offer, one action. Remove the main navigation or reduce it to the logo and the CTA. Hero with the promise and the action, then only what removes doubt: the mechanism, the boundary (what is not promised), proof, the form. Repeat the primary CTA no more than every viewport and a half. Match the page to the ad that sent the visitor: same promise, same words, same offer. Add `noindex` for ad-only pages when the brief says so.

## Product page blueprint (RHAD45)

Follow `rhad45-web.md`: hero with the four equal products and the price line, the kinetic or monument moment, the studio showing a labelled example working, one price section, our work (agency credentials, labelled), review form, FAQ, footer.

## Grant or programme page blueprint (EDGE-style)

Monument hero, a "what changed" explanation, a route finder (if your business needs to... ), a conditional cost illustration labelled "Examples only. Prices exclude GST." where true, packages, an eligibility checklist, the process slip with who owns each step (the applicant submits; RHAD does not), FAQ, coupon form, cropped wordmark footer. Every claim is conditional ("where eligible", "activity-specific"). Never "guaranteed", "approved vendor", "apply now", "secure funding", "claim grant". Credit photographs.

## Homepage blueprint (RHAD)

Monument hero stating what RHAD is for in one line, a split statement on the way RHAD works (commercial intent first), services as a definition list that links to service pages, selected work with permission, a plain "start a conversation" section, footer. Route visitors, do not recite capabilities.

## Section checklist (apply to every section)

- What is its job in one sentence?
- Whose words are the headline: the customer's situation or our jargon?
- What real content and proof does it use?
- Does anything above or below it already say this?
- What is its composition, and is that different from its neighbours?
- What does the visitor do next from here?

## Conversion path rules

- One primary action. Every CTA label is the same and names the outcome ("Arrange my review").
- CTAs go to the form or the action, not to a section.
- Product or option buttons pre-fill the matching form field and announce it. They never auto-submit.
- Say what happens next and what does not.
- The form is as short as the next step allows. Mobile number and context optional.
- Confirm with the real endpoint before showing success.

## Existing page redesign

Capture before touching: screenshots at five widths, the current copy, analytics if the user can share them, the current form flow. Keep what converts and what is true. Change one level at a time (structure, then composition, then craft) so the user can critique each. List removed content and why in `handoff.md`.
